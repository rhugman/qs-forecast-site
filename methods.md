# Methods and data

Issued 2026-09-10 (run 2026-09-10T08:26:28Z, code 8f7b90c (dirty tree)). 15 product sites of 18 candidates; horizon 2026-09-10 to 2027-03-11 (183 days). This page is written by `scripts/09_report.py` from the run tables; every number on it is read from them.

## What this is

A research demonstration, non-commercial and unofficial: season-ahead forecasts of groundwater level (head, m above sea level) at SNIRH monitoring wells of the Querença-Silves aquifer (Algarve, Portugal). One transfer-function-noise model per well (pastas 2.0.0), driven by daily rain, reference evapotranspiration and pumping, forecast from ensembles of weather forecasts and the irrigation demand they imply. Two seasons a year, 1 April to 30 September (drawdown) and 1 October to 31 March (recovery), re-issued weekly. The output is a probability distribution of head by day: model output, not a measurement, and not a decision product. Nothing here is a warning, a permit condition or advice, and no warranty is given.

Product sites (15): 595/212, 595/215, 595/309, 595/1016, 595/1024, 595/1025, 595/1028, 595/1033, 596/35, 596/259, 596/262, 597/96, 597/111, 597/121, 597/364. The 3 other candidates are run and shown but did not pass the hindcast gate (Verification).

## Data

**Heads.** SNIRH manual dips (APA), 1978-03-09 to 2026-07-28 (28600 rows over all wells). A well is a candidate when its SNIRH series passes QC, has at least 200 dips since 2000-01-01, was read in 2026 and has no gap longer than 730 days; the product sites hold 263 to 578 dips. Calibration windows start between 2000-01-03 and 2003-02-01 and run to the last dip.

**Age of the last head observation.** SNIRH publishes with a lag of one to two months, so the dip a forecast is anchored on is old: at this issue the last dips of the product sites date from 2026-06-29 to 2026-07-06, 65 to 72 days before the issue date. The age is stated on every site page and enters the spread (Uncertainty).

**Weather record.** ERA5-Land daily rain and FAO-56 reference ET0 (Copernicus Climate Change Service, retrieved through Google Earth Engine), bias-corrected to Algarve gauges in the M5 repository and weighted over the 12 in-domain cells to one domain series (320 km2), 1980-01-01 to 2026-08-10. At issue that record was 31 days old; it was extended to 2026-09-09 with Open-Meteo ERA5 scaled to it by monthly ratio (25 days) and the EC46 control run (5 days).

**Pumping.** Águas do Algarve's public supply from the aquifer, metered monthly 2009-01 to 2026-05, enters as a domain aggregate (no per-licence data is carried); over the horizon it takes the calendar-month climatology of the last 36 reported months. Irrigation has monthly returns for 2024, 2025 only: what the reporting titles declared, a floor on the groundwater draw, not the draw itself. Two weather-driven representations are offered to the models, one per site at most: the lagged-deficit proxy, 0.0382 x max(2.0 ET0 - P, 0) on days of year 1-202, lagged 85 days, fitted on those 24 months (RMSE 0.71 hm3/month, r 0.90, September peak reproduced in both years), and the crop-water balance below. Each weather member drives its own demand. Arade reservoir storage (SNIRH 30G/02A, 1955 to 2026-08) is carried in the stress table and used by no model.

**Irrigation demand (crop-water balance).** The `irrig_cwb` stress comes from a daily root-zone bucket per crop group over the 6,824 ha of IFAP 2024 parcels flagged irrigated (8,244 parcels, aggregated by group in M5; no parcel is carried here): rain is stored up to the depletion, the crop empties the bucket at Kc(month) x ET0, irrigation replaces each day what the crop used once the depletion exceeds p x TAW (the domain aggregate of parcels irrigated on different days; refilling each bucket to capacity instead changes the timing, not the total: +10 % on the annual mean), gross = net / efficiency, volume = depth x area. Kc, root depth, p and efficiency are FAO-56 values (Tables 12, 19 and 22; drip 0.85, sprinkler 0.75; available water 120 mm per m of root zone), not fitted; the dry orchards (carob, olive, almond) carry a deficit-irrigation coefficient of 0.45-0.55.

The level the aquifer supplies is a share of that gross and cannot be identified from the heads (the model gain absorbs it), so three levels are carried: the reporting floor (share 0.37, reproduces the 2024, 2025 totals), the inferred level (share 0.98: M5's estimate of reported titles plus the IFAP duty of the non-reporting ones over the bucket's all-parcel gross of 2025) and every parcel on groundwater (share 1.0). The stress column uses the inferred level, which rests on M5's inference (scripts/05b_build_abstraction.py); the other two bound it. The returns check the shape, not the level: the bucket's monthly volumes correlate with the 2024, 2025 returns at r 0.90 as computed (RMSE 0.70 hm3/month after scaling to the reported total) and 0.90 shifted 30 days; the best shift is 15 days (r 0.95). The returns peak in month 9, the bucket in 7: part of that is read as a reporting lag (a meter read at the turn of the month lands the previous weeks' water in the next return), the rest, with the 10 % of the returns that fall in November-March and the bucket does not produce, is unexplained. Interannually: Spearman rho 0.42 (p 0.035) between the calendar-year all-parcel demand and the remote-sensing count of 500 m pixels with a growing-season ETa deficit above 50 mm over 2000-2024 (the proxy: 0.33).

## Model

pastas 2.0.0, one model per site at a daily step, every simulation warmed up from 1980-01-01: a RechargeModel on P and ET0 with a Gamma or Exponential response and Linear recharge (P - f ET0) or the non-linear FlexModel root-zone bucket; optional StressModels for the irrigation proxy or the crop-water-balance demand (one of the two at most) and the public supply (Exponential response, downward), or pumping folded into the recharge term as negative recharge (Pumping, below; exclusive with the separate terms and with FlexModel); an AR(1) noise model. The structure is chosen by forward selection from the Gamma + Linear baseline: a departure qualifies when it lowers the out-of-sample residual variance in both halves of the calibration window (fit on one, score on the other) and every pumping term the candidate holds has the expected sign with |gain| > 2 stderr in the whole-window fit; information criteria are recorded, not used. 549 specs were tried (24 to 41 per site). FlexModel is kept only when identified, every recharge parameter off its bounds with a standard error below its value: it improved both halves at 7 sites (595/1016, 595/1024, 595/1028, 595/1033, 595/212, 597/362, 597/96) but was identified at none; sites carrying it: none, the rest fell back to Linear. Chosen structures: Gamma+Linear at 9 sites, Gamma+Linear+netpump8+fp at 3 sites, Gamma+Linear+netpump6+fp at 1 site, Exponential+Linear+netpump4+fp at 1 site, Exponential+Linear+netpump8+fp at 1 site, Gamma+Linear+netpump6+fp+sw-arade-1 at 1 site, Gamma+Linear+netpump6+fp+ada12 at 1 site, Gamma+Linear+netpump4+fp at 1 site. Pumping terms kept: 595/212 net pumping (m 6, see Pumping), 595/215 net pumping (m 4, see Pumping), 595/309 net pumping (m 8, see Pumping), 595/1016 net pumping (m 8, see Pumping), 595/1024 net pumping (m 6, see Pumping), 595/1025 net pumping (m 8, see Pumping), 595/1028 net pumping (m 6, see Pumping), 595/1033 net pumping (m 8, see Pumping), 596/262 net pumping (m 4, see Pumping); the other 9 sites have no identifiable pumping signal in their dips. R2 of the product fits: 0.68 to 0.82.

## Pumping

**Pumping as negative recharge.** A well field draws on the storage that recharge fills, so in a lumped aquifer the head response to a millimetre pumped is minus the response to a millimetre recharged: the same response function and gain, opposite sign. Besides the separate irrigation and public-supply terms above, every site is therefore offered a net-recharge formulation in which the recharge model's precipitation becomes P - m x pump, with pump the crop-water-balance irrigation draw at the inferred share plus the public supply as a depth over the 320 km2 domain. The multiplier m is chosen per site from 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 by the same split-sample rule (the model with the term must lower the out-of-sample residual variance in both halves of the record against the model without it; ties go to the smaller m); it is not fitted jointly with the gain, and the separate terms compete in the same selection, so the table says which representation the dips prefer where. m = 1 says a millimetre pumped costs the well a millimetre of recharge; m > 1 that the wells sit nearer the pumping than the recharge (the spatial concentration is folded into m), m < 1 the opposite; the unknown level of the irrigation draw is folded into m as well, and the formulation carries no delay between pumping and head beyond the recharge response. The choice rests on the heads and the split sample alone; no groundwater-flow model enters. Result: net pumping at 9 sites (595/212 (m 6), 595/215 (m 4), 595/309 (m 8), 595/1016 (m 8), 595/1024 (m 6), 595/1025 (m 8), 595/1028 (m 6), 595/1033 (m 8), 596/262 (m 4)), a separate term at 0 (none), no pumping term at 9. **Footprint level, wellfield weight, drought switching.** The stress table applies the 2024 irrigated footprint to every year, while the remote-sensing extent index of the irrigated area rose by about half after 2017, so three additions to the pumping block are offered under the same split-sample rule (docs/plan.md step 15), each taken only where it lowers the out-of-sample residual variance in both halves: the irrigation part of the pumping times the footprint level (rs_level: the 3-year trailing mean of the extent index over its 2022-2024 mean, 0.54 to 1.10 over 2000-2024 and 1.0 over any horizon, so the forecast draw and the scenarios do not change through it; in the hindcast the level at an origin is the mean of the index over the three index years before the origin year), a weight m_a of its own on the public supply (grid 3, 6, 12, 24; the two wellfields lie 1-3 km from the western wells, where a domain-average depth understates them), and a drought-switching term beta x all-parcel demand x s (beta from 1, 2) with s the Arade storage deficit (1 - S / 20 hm3, clipped to [0, 1]; over a horizon the last observed storage plus the climatological monthly change) or the relative 24-month rain deficit (from each member's own rain): groundwater replacing surface water when reservoir allocations are cut. The switch term reads the all-parcel demand, so it is the same in every irrigation scenario. Result: the footprint level at 9 of the 9 net-pumping sites (it beat the constant level at 7 of the 7 sites where both qualified), m_a different from m at 1, a switch at 1 (1 Arade, 0 24-month rain); m sits at the top of the grid (8) at 4 site(s) (595/309, 595/1016, 595/1025, 595/1033). What m means with these forms: a lumped scale between a domain-average millimetre pumped and a millimetre of recharge lost at the well, which also absorbs the level of the inferred irrigation share (the heads cannot identify the share) and, with the footprint level, the growth the index carries; in the fit it trades against the ET factor f, because the summer shapes of ET0 and pumping compete for the same drawdown, which is why the split sample and not the fit decides. What remains: the 2005 and 2018 summers are closer to the dips (Jun-Sep residuals -0.6 and +1.4 residual sd, mean over the net-pumping sites), but the drought summers of 2023 and 2024 stay simulated too high at 9 of those sites (-1.8 and -1.7 residual sd on average, range -2.6 to -1.2; each model's own residual sd): the footprint level fits the 2010-2019 decade, not the recent drought summers; a non-linear recharge under pumping is a candidate, not built. ## Stresses in the forecast

Weather members: ECMWF EC46 (51 members, complete to 2026-10-24) spliced member-by-member onto ECMWF SEAS5 (51 members, to 2027-03-11), both daily rain and FAO-56 ET0 from the Open-Meteo Seasonal API at ~36 km (fetched 2026-09-10 08:21 UTC; the control run kept as a member; join after 2026-10-24). Open-Meteo keeps members for a month, so every run is archived under data/feed. Per member: irrigation from its own P and ET0 through the proxy (the lag reaches back into the observed record; none) or through the crop-water balance run forward from the soil state at the end of the observed record (none); public supply the climatology above, identical in every member. A coverage guard runs before every simulation: a stress that ends early is an error, never silently padded.

Correction status: none (config/feeds.yml: none). The members are not bias-corrected: no maps under data/derived/bias-correction. The correction is fitted on the C3S hindcasts on the Climate Data Store, which need a token this machine does not have; the 8 candidate systems (ecmwf SEAS5.1, meteo_france System 9, dwd GCFS 2.2, dwd GCFS 2.1, cmcc SPS 4, eccc CanESM5.1, ukmo GloSea6-GC3.2 (system 604), ukmo GloSea6-GC5.1) are therefore not assessed: no CDS token (scripts/04_assess_feeds.py stops at the token gate). ## Uncertainty

The band pools three sources: the 51 weather members, 10 parameter sets per member drawn from the solver covariance, and the residual persistence, an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck noise started from the last residual at the anchor with tau and variance fitted to the residual autocorrelation of each site (20 paths per member x parameter path), its standard deviation multiplied by a per-site inflation factor k (below). The variance is weather + parameter + noise by the law of total variance; the quantiles come from the mixture of all paths, so a skewed weather ensemble keeps its shape. The anchor age adds to the lead inside the noise term: an older dip widens the band and weakens the pull of the last observation. pastas' own forecast variance is undefined for irregularly observed series, which is why the noise term is built here from the residuals.

**Noise inflation.** The noise term is fitted to the residual autocorrelation, not to forecast errors, and with k = 1 the anchored band under-covered in the hindcast (trajectory 90 % coverage 0.89 over all sites, 50 % coverage 0.55, PIT variance 0.066 against 0.083 for a uniform PIT). Per site, k is the factor on the noise standard deviation that gives the standardised errors of the anchor-age-0 ESP trajectory dips (both origins, every lead in the window, 253 to 420 dips per site) unit variance: mean(e^2 / (var_weather + var_parameter + k^2 var_noise)) = 1, e = dip minus forecast mean, solved by root finding and clipped to 0.5 to 3.0 (0 at a bound: none). The scores below are honest to that fit: every scored band is drawn with the leave-one-year-out k, estimated on the other origin years and applied to the held-out year; the published forecast uses the full-sample k of its site (0.66 to 1.81, product median 1.01), stored in the fit anchor and shown in the site JSON. The weather members are ESP: the observed years resampled with the target year left out. The operational SEAS5 and EC46 members have no hindcast here (no token, see above), so these scores measure the model with a climatological weather ensemble, not the feed; a real feed may add to them or subtract from them, and the known-forcing run below bounds what it could add. ### The 1 April conditional correction

The known-forcing run still misses the season-end lows after dry winters at the western wells (Limitations), and the miss is partly a function of the state already known at the origin: the winter's rain, the Arade storage, the footprint level, the anchor's own residual and its percentile. A ridge regression of the standardised season-end error on those seven (rain6_anom, rain12_anom, arade_anom, rs_level, anchor_pct, e0, e0_prev) - and a second of its spread - is fitted on the hindcast (2001-2025) at the nodes 1 Apr, 1 Jun, 1 Aug, pooled over the west sector with one intercept per site, and written to `config/postprocess.json`; the weekly job interpolates the coefficient vector linearly by issue date between those nodes and the zero vector on the season-end target day, shifts every quantile by the standardised bias times the band's own width on the day and scales the deviations from the mean by the multiplier, floored at 1.0 so it cannot narrow the band. Not applied to: the east (the correction gains nothing there: 1 Apr season-end CRPSS 0.540 -> 0.531 leave-one-year-out), 596/259 (sector `uncertain`: one well, so a per-sector fit does no pooling - below MIN_NODE_SITES = 2; its leave-one-year-out fit gained +0.099 at 1 April and is an open item, not a product form), a 1 October issue (0.325 -> 0.260, it loses), any date before 1 April or at and after the season-end target. Leave-one-origin-year-out over the 9 corrected sites at the 1 April origin (595-1016, 595-1024, 595-1025, 595-1028, 595-1033, 595-212, 595-215, 595-309, 596-262), season end: median CRPSS 0.603 to 0.703, median absolute error 1.88 m to 0.95 m, 90 % coverage 0.80 to 0.90 against a nominal 0.90; `scores.csv` and the skill page carry both bands and the gate above is on the uncorrected one. Refitted in the operational order instead - each year from the years strictly before it, 183 season-end origins from 2012 on - the gain holds: CRPSS 0.588 raw, 0.649 forward-only against 0.654 leave-one-year-out (bootstrap difference 0.060), and on the 43 season-end origins of the 2022-2024 drought block, forecast from a fit that ends in 2021, CRPSS goes 0.673 to 0.755 and the median absolute error 2.60 m to 2.05 m. The gain decays as the season closes and the unadjusted forecast improves (1 Apr 0.122, 1 Jun 0.044, 1 Aug 0.027 of CRPSS at the three nodes), which is what the interpolation to zero encodes. At this issue it is applied at 9 sites with node weights 1 Aug 0.34: 595-1016 0.43 m x1.00, 595-1024 0.49 m x1.00, 595-1025 0.46 m x1.00, 595-1028 0.31 m x1.00, 595-1033 0.32 m x1.00, 595-212 0.32 m x1.00, 595-215 0.12 m x1.00, 595-309 0.18 m x1.00, 596-262 0.35 m x1.00. The correction is statistical: it carries no forcing, and outside the origin states the record holds it extrapolates linearly.

## Limitations

- The weather feeds are coarse next to the aquifer: ~36 km cells from Open-Meteo and 1 deg products on the Climate Data Store against a 320 km2 domain. The usable signal is the seasonal anomaly; a bias correction fixes the climatology, not the skill.
- The irrigation level is not identifiable from the heads: the crop-water balance is carried at M5's inferred share (0.98 of the all-parcel demand) between the reporting floor (0.37) and every parcel on groundwater (1.0); the proxy's shape rests on 2 years of monthly returns (2024, 2025), and the returns' September peak is only partly explained.
- Pumping is identified at 9 of 18 sites (9 as negative recharge, 0 as a separate term); elsewhere the dips do not separate it from recharge and it stays in the residual. Where it is, m folds the well-field geometry and the irrigation level together, trades against the ET factor in the fit and carries no delay of its own; the footprint level, the AdA weight and the drought switch are chosen by the same split sample, and the 2023-2024 drought summers remain simulated too high where a term is kept.
- Residual persistence exceeds one year at 6 of 18 sites (west: 130 to 880 d, near a unit root over a 183-day season), so western bands are wide and the lower quantile can reach below sea level, which the aquifer does not; at this issue that happens at none (no site).
- The members are uncorrected (correction status none) and the feed's own skill is untested here; the hindcast skill is that of the model with a climatological ensemble.
- The historic-input realisations are diagnostic; what the ensemble misses stays in the noise term (pastas has no errors-in-variables estimator).
- Candidate sites are shown with their own bands but carry no skill claim.
